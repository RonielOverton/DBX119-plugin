# dbx 119 — research notes

What the plugin is modeled on, with the source for each fact. Items marked **unverified** need the manual or measurements from the owner's unit (the owner has a working dbx 119; see `tools/calibration/`).

Source quality: **[M]** = original dbx manual text, **[F]** = forum post by a technician or owner, **[S]** = seller or review site.

## What it is

- Stereo dB-linear compressor/expander from dbx, sold from 1976, a modification of the dbx 117. Stereo RCA in and out (unbalanced, consumer level). [S] ([audiocostruzioni](https://audiocostruzioni.com/en/prodotto/dbx-119/), [eBay](https://www.ebay.com/itm/117260216091))
- The 117 manual has a supplement for the 119; the schematics are the same except for the changes in the supplement. [F] ([groupdiy](https://groupdiy.com/threads/dbx-119.14522/))
- No separate 119 schematic is known to exist. One owner traced the differences by hand: a threshold diode and an extra op-amp (called OA5), which appears to drive the threshold LED. [F] ([groupdiy](https://groupdiy.com/threads/dbx-119-schematic-or-pictures-of-the-board.75310/))

## Inside the unit

- Very simple: an op-amp, one RMS detector module, VCA modules, a few capacitors and a transformer; no voltage regulators. [F] ([gearspace](https://gearspace.com/board/geekzone/89931-looking-dbx-119-schematic.html))
- Modules: one RMS detector and two VCAs. [F] ([gearspace](https://gearspace.com/board/geekzone/109633-dbx-128-a.html)) One detector for two VCAs means the two channels share one gain control signal: **stereo-linked detection** (supports the plugin default; confirm with the stereo link test).
- The 117/119 use the dbx 200 VCA (silver can) and the 208 RMS detector. [F] ([reviveaudio](https://www.reviveaudio.com/index.php?route=product%2Fproduct&product_id=369), [mixing-mastering-online](https://www.mixing-mastering-online.com/single-post/dbx-compressors-what-do-they-sound-like-and-how-do-they-work))
- The 200 VCA is a seven-transistor Blackmer gain cell with a -6 mV/dB control law ("decilinear"). [S] ([Mix](https://www.mixonline.com/?p=108887))
- A dbx employee (quoted) said the 117/118/119 share the same VCA as the 160VU but different input/output circuits, since the 160VU is balanced and these are unbalanced. Another poster disputes that the 118 uses the same VCA. [F] ([gearspace](https://gearspace.com/board/geekzone/109633-dbx-128-a.html))
- In the 119, the VCA input comes from the unbalanced input through a 220 pF cap to ground, a 0.22 µF cap and a 100 kΩ resistor. [F] ([groupdiy](https://groupdiy.com/threads/output-level-modification-ideas-d-b-x-119-schematic-included.34377/))
- The control voltage is scaled by a 24.9 kΩ / 200 Ω divider, giving about ±0.12 V, which at 6 mV/dB is about **±20 dB of gain range**. [F] (same thread) The plugin currently allows +24 / -60 dB; this should be checked against the static curve measurements.
- Someone modified a 119 with a feed-forward / feedback switch, which means the detector position is a real design choice. Which one the stock unit uses is **unverified**. The plugin is feed-forward. [F] (same thread)

## Controls

|---|---|---|
| Compress/Expand (large knob) | 117: from 1.4 compression slope (14 dB out per 20 dB in, slope 0.7) to 2.0 expansion slope. 119: compression up to infinity:1. At 1.0 the signal passes unchanged. | [M] ([audioservicemanuals](https://www.audioservicemanuals.com/d/dbx/dbx-117/4764-dbx-117-119-owners-manual)), [tonbandmuseum](http://www.tonbandmuseum.info/dbx-dbx-117-119.html) |

| Red LED | Lights when the level exceeds the threshold. | [vintagedigital](https://vintagedigital.com.au/?p=166983) |

## Timing (117 manual specifications) [M]

- Attack: 12 ms to 85 % of the final value, at any compression or expansion setting.
- Release: constant rate, 140 dB/s (Norm) or 50 dB/s (Slow), measured at 1.4 expansion. The 119 has Norm only.
- Dynamic range 120 dB; frequency response +0/-1 dB 20 Hz–20 kHz; max input over 30 V RMS; input impedance 50 kΩ; output 10 V RMS max, output impedance 500 Ω.

Source: OCR of the owner's manual on [audioservicemanuals](https://www.audioservicemanuals.com/d/dbx/dbx-117/4764-dbx-117-119-owners-manual). The OCR has errors, so these numbers need checking against the scan.

**Conflict:** tonbandmuseum gives the release as "up to 180 dB/s"; the manual gives 140 dB/s at 1.4 expansion. If 140 dB/s is the output change at slope 1.4, the detector itself falls at about 140 / 1.4 = 100 dB/s, or 140 / 0.4 = 350 dB/s if it is the gain change. The plugin uses 180 dB/s at the detector for now. **The attack/release measurement on the owner's unit decides this.**

## How the model maps this

See `src/dsp/CompanderCore.h`:

- Gain (dB) = (slope − 1) × (level − threshold). LINEAR uses the full range; ABOVE THRESHOLD uses only the part above the threshold.
- Detector: one-pole mean-square integrator (12 ms), then the dB level may not fall faster than 180 dB/s. Feed-forward, stereo-linked by default.
- Safety limits (assumption): +24 dB max boost, -60 dB max cut, detector floor -80 dBFS. Likely to become ±20 dB after measurement.
- Test kit results for the model itself (2026-11-17): the gain reaches 85 % of a 30 dB step in about **5 ms**, faster than the manual's 12 ms, because the 12 ms integrator works on power and the 85 % point is read in dB. Release measured exactly 180 dB/s at the detector (90 dB/s of gain at 2:1). Detector ripple at 1 kHz gives about 0.08 % THD (3rd harmonic), close to the published 0.1 % typical. All of these are to be compared with the owner's unit.
- Not modeled yet: VCA coloration and noise; the real detector's program-dependent behavior; the plugin's threshold range (-60 to 0 dBFS) is not mapped to the hardware's markings.
